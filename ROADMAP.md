# EngineeringTools roadmap: Eurocode (UK NA) calculation coverage

Target scope: the structural calculations in the public Tekla Tedds UK Eurocode library list
([support.tekla.com, 14 Oct 2025](https://support.tekla.com/doc/tekla-tedds/not-version-specific/lib_calculation_list_eurocodes)),
rebuilt independently as self-contained EngineeringTools modules.

## Clean-room rules

- **Scope** comes from the public calculation list only: names, not methods.
- **Methods** come from BS EN 1990 to 1997, their UK National Annexes, and published guidance (SCI, Concrete Centre, TRADA, IStructE), with clause references in the output.
- **Not used as input:** Tedds software, help text, report layouts, screenshots or outputs. Decompiling or reverse engineering Tedds is prohibited by the Trimble Tekla EULA (2024) cl. 4.11(d). Using Tedds to develop other software is prohibited by cl. 4.11(f).
- **Verification** is against published worked examples, closed-form results and independent reference implementations, kept as runnable tests under `tests/`.

## Scope

The UK list has 91 entries. Out of scope:

- 5 civils entries: drain and sewer design, open channel flow, stormwater drainage, cut and fill, slope stability.
- 4 non-calculation or proprietary entries: Tata Steel RoofDek, batch design, section sketch, co-ordinate conversion.

That leaves **82 structural entries**. Many share one engine. For example, the four steel member entries are a single EC3 member check behind different front ends.

## Interface

All pages share one light style (`common/style.css` in the build sources): IBM Plex Sans, ink on a blue-grey ground with white cards, one blue (#1d5fbf) on the main data (envelopes, bars, utilisation), green or red only on the verdict and on a check that fails, and a near-black primary button. Each tool's title row has its own photograph of the work it checks (`img/`, from Wikimedia Commons, set per page with `--photo` and credited in the row's corner and in `img/CREDITS.md`); a page without one falls back to its material's photograph, and without the image folder to a plain dark band. The printed A4 calculation sheets are unchanged. The home page banner cycles twelve of the photographs (steel, concrete, masonry and timber) behind the title, a slow cross-fade every 6 s with each photograph fetched one slide ahead (a still picture for readers who prefer less motion), then the search, the material tabs and the lists by material. A narrated tour (`media/engineering-tools-tour.mp4`, about 3 minutes with captions) opens from a thumbnail in the banner, or a button on narrow screens; it plays in a dialog and loads only when asked for. Its music is an original cue, the narration Kokoro-82M (Apache-2.0).

Every tool has **Open** and **Save** at the top right of its title row (below the title on phones; Ctrl+O / Ctrl+S; a file can also be dropped onto the page). Save writes the tool's inputs, project details included, to a `.json` file: `{ app: 'EngineeringTools', tool, key, format: 1, saved, state }`, with a Save As dialog in Chrome and Edge and a download elsewhere, named from the job no. and member ref. (for example `24-123 B1-3 Steel Member Design.json`). Open checks the file is from the same tool (a file from another tool is refused, naming that tool) and recalculates. Tools that keep their inputs in browser storage open a file by writing its state there and reloading, so each tool rebuilds its load tables, spans and models through its own start-up code; the 2D Frame applies the model in place so Undo still works, and the four pages without stored state (Steel Connection Design, RC Column, RC Crack Width, Concrete Tie Force) restore their fields directly, dropdowns first so a section's auto-filled dimensions do not overwrite saved values. The shared code is one block (`common/fileio.css`, `common/fileio.js` in the build sources) placed in each page by `fileio/inject.py`; `tests/fileio/test_fileio.js` checks the wiring on every tool, and a Playwright run saves, reopens in a fresh browser and compares the state on all 35.

**Drawings (November 2026).** Composite Beam Design, Floor Vibration, Steel Member Design, Concrete Tie Force and RC Crack Width now draw the arrangement, not only the results: the composite section with the plastic stress blocks and neutral axis, the studs at their pitch and the effective width; the floor plan with the effective floor area L_eff × S, both mode shapes with their frequencies and the beam section; the member with its axial force, end-moment diagrams, buckling lengths and LT segments; the tie layout on the column grid (with the vertical-tie section for classes 2B and 3); and the wall elevation with its restraints and the predicted crack pattern at s_r,max, with the section and h_c,eff. Each is drawn to scale from the inputs with the JD drafting kit (`joints/diagrams.js`), `drawings/kit.js` and one module per tool in the build sources; the governing check or location is red. They sit under the verdict, redraw as the inputs change, and go on the A4 sheet or the PDF. `tests/drawings/test_drawings.js` sweeps each tool's input variations for missing or invalid drawings. Second group, also November 2026: Steel Beam Torsion (the beam with its torsion end conditions and eccentric loads, the section with the shear centre and the load line, and the flange stresses at the critical section); Beam Web Openings (the span with the openings numbered and their M and V marked, and the governing opening or web-post at true scale with its tee forces); Steel Frame Design (the frame with its loads, restraints, member sections and, after a Check, each member coloured by utilisation); Sheet Pile Wall (ground, water and soil layers each side, the prop force, the equilibrium and required toes and any toe provided, the pressures with their values and the bending moment with its maximum); the RC Section Toolkit's torsion (t_ef, A_k, u_k and the shear flow), crack width (A_c,eff, the strains and the cracks at s_r,max) and anchorage tabs (l_bd beyond the section, the lap with its transverse zones); RC Column (each axis with its end conditions, l_0 and buckled shape, λ against λ_lim, the moments M01, M02, M0e and M2, and the candidate design moments against M_Rd; also in the PDF); Hollowcore Support Beam (the plank bearing with e, the bearing length and the lever, the construction stages with their torque, and the elevation with the bending moments); Steel Masonry Support (one view per support type, with the fixing or weld forces and the toe deflection); and Steel Member Fire Design (exposure, protection and the heated perimeter for A_m/V, k_y,θ against temperature with the design point, and the heating curves). Found on the way and fixed: the Sheet Pile Wall embedment check compared the toe provided below the formation with the requirement below the overdig level, so it missed the unplanned excavation; a stainless plate under a beam in Steel Masonry Support had no weld resistance (β_w is now 1.0, BS EN 1993-1-4 4.3(4)) and its verdict could show PASS; Steel Beam Torsion now builds in the root when a cantilever is chosen. Still to add: the additions to ten more tools are listed in `docs/plans/drawings-plan.md` (done, below). Bolt Group Analysis now draws the bolt pattern to scale with the pitch and edge chains, the centroid, the force on its line of action and its eccentricity, the moment about the centroid and each bolt's shear as a vector (the governing bolt red), and an edge-on view of the bolt tensions by row when there is tension or moment. Steel Column Design draws the column with its restraints, buckling lengths, loads and moment diagrams, and the plan at the cap with the beam reactions 100 mm from the faces and the nominal moments they make; Base Plate Design draws the plate, welds and anchors (the joints kit's view) and how the base works: the effective bearing area in plan and the component model in elevation (bearing at f_jd, the anchor row in tension, z_C and z_T). Steel Beam Analysis and Design draws the beam as it is entered: the loads stacked by case with their values, the supports, the lateral restraints and the LT segments (each with its utilisation after a Check), the location of the governing check in red, the deflected shape of each member under the pattern of variable load that deflects it most (with Q alone against L/limit) and the section with its properties; the elevation redraws as the inputs change. RC Beam Analysis and Design draws the design section at each support (hogging, the worse side) and each mid-span (sagging) to scale: the flange to b_eff (broken where it is wide), the links, the tension bars by layer and any compression bars, the neutral axis and compression block from the bars provided, M against M_Rd, d, x, the span/depth check and the zone's utilisation. Third group (plan in `docs/plans/drawings-plan.md`): Windpost Design draws the post in the face of the wall with the panel it carries (half each side), the floors or base, the head and base fixings and the ties at their spacing, and beside it on the same height scale the ULS wind with the reactions, the bending moment with M_Ed against its resistance and the deflected shape against its limit, then the section with the axis it bends about; RC Stair Design draws the flight to scale with every dimension (projections, landings, goings, span, rise, waist and landing thicknesses), the loads on plan for each part, the bottom bars along the soffit at their cover and any top bars at the supports, the bending moment of the governing pattern with each part's moment, and a row per part with d, M_Ed against M_Rd and its worst check (the half landing likewise, with the flights' loads where they bear); the RC Section Toolkit's section design tab draws the section to scale beside its strain diagram (ε_cu2, ε_s, ε_s2) and its stress block and forces with the lever arm z and M_Rd = F_c z + F_s2 (d − d_2), the shear truss to scale with the links, the struts at θ and the chords at z with V_Ed against V_Rd,c, V_Rd,s and V_Rd,max, and the span/depth ratio against the allowed value with its factors. RC Punching Shear (and the critical column in RC Flat Slab Design) draws the plan with u_0, u_1 at 2d and u_out,ef labelled and the link perimeters with every leg, beside the stress and resistance at each perimeter; the section through the slab with the column, the top bars, d, the 26.6° cone to u_1, the links with s_0 and s_r, and the last perimeter within 1.5d of u_out,ef; and the shear stresses against v_Rd,max, v_Rd,c, 2v_Rd,c and v_Rd,cs. RC Flat Slab Design's elevation now gives the loads, and its strip plan the column and middle strip widths with the top bars over each column and the bottom bars of each span in each strip. Each redraws as the inputs change. Snow Loading draws each load case above the roof section, both to scale: the load in kN/m² with μ where it changes, the extent of each part (a drift's l_s marked), and the roof's slopes, step, parapet and obstruction heights and widths. Beam Analysis's model gives every load with its case and value (distributed loads stacked so none overlap, points, couples, upward wind) and, after a run, the ULS reaction under each support with any uplift in red. Wind Loading's plan gives each roof zone its c_pe, each wall zone A to E its c_pe and w_e, and the zone extents from e dimensioned; the windward face gives q_p for each strip and the overall force at the height of its resultant with the overturning moment; both are now on the A4 sheet for every direction. RC Wall Design draws the wall panel in elevation: the clear height and thickness to scale, the floors with k₁ and k₂ (or pinned ends), braced or sway, the vertical bars, N_Ed and the end moments, l₀ with the buckled shape, and beside it the moments up the wall (M01, M02 with the imperfection, M0e, M2 at mid-height or at the end when unbraced, and the minimum e₀N_Ed) with λ against λ_lim and M_Ed against M_Rd at N_Ed; a plain wall shows its eccentricities, β from Table 12.1, Φ and N_Rd. It redraws as the inputs change; the core-wall plan now gives each core's share of the NS and EW wind. Section Properties draws the shape with its overall size and the centroid dimensioned from the left and the bottom, the elastic axes through the centroid, the plastic neutral axes where they differ, the shear centre and its offset from the centroid, and the principal axes at α, with the values that place them beside it. That completes the third group. RC Beam Analysis and Design, RC Slab Design and RC Flat Slab Design now draw from the inputs as soon as the page opens and redraw as they change (before, the drawings came only with Calculate): the elevation with each load labelled by case and value, the bending moment and shear envelopes, the design sections, the strip plan and the punching views; Calculate adds the verdict, the checks and the tables under them. RC Slab Design adds per-metre sections at each support (the worse side) and each mid-span, and for a two-way panel mid-panel both ways and at each continuous edge: 600 mm of the slab to scale with the bars the section cuts at their spacing and layer, the bars across them, the compression block and neutral axis from the bars provided, M_Ed against M_Rd, h, d, x, the span/depth check and the zone's utilisation; they are on the A4 sheet too.

Every page carries the disclaimer at its foot, and every printed calculation sheet and PDF report ends with it: the tools are an aid to design without warranty, the author accepts no responsibility for errors in them or their results or for any use made of them, and a competent structural engineer with relevant experience must check and approve all calculations before use.

Status key: **Done** means it's in this repo with tests; how far those tests reach is given under Verification status, and a Done tool marked partly or not independently verified has a gap still to close. **Partial** means an existing tool covers part of it. **Planned** means it isn't started. The home page lists every Planned tool, in the build plan or not yet scheduled, with a Planned tag.

## Verification status (November 2026)

Every live tool has tests, but not every tool has been checked against an independent published source. A tool is **Verified** when its main calculation path reproduces published results: The Concrete Centre's spreadsheets, worked examples in published guides and textbooks, or exact closed-form solutions for analysis. It is **Partly verified** when only part of it has been checked that way. It is **Not independently verified** when it has been checked only against hand calculations or a Python reference written alongside it. Those catch coding mistakes but not a misreading of the code, because they share the same reading. **No calculation tests** means nothing checks the page's numbers at all.

Of the 63 live tools, 27 are verified, 18 partly verified (Steel Member Design and Steel Beam Analysis and Design share one engine and one row) and 18 not independently verified; every live tool now has calculation tests. The 36 tools that are not fully verified are flagged in the tables below and should be used with extra care until their gap is closed:
Steel Member Design; Steel Beam Analysis and Design, Steel Column Design, Base Plate Design, Steel Frame Design, Steel Connection Design (ConnectionCheck), Bolt Group Analysis, Composite Beam Design, Beam Web Openings, Steel Beam Torsion, Hollowcore Support Beam, Steel Member Fire Design, Floor Vibration, RC Slab FE Design, RC Corbel and Nib Design, RC Lintel Design, RC Wall Crack Control (RC Crack Width), Precast Connections, Steel Masonry Support, Windpost Design, Masonry Bearing, Masonry Column, Masonry Wall Panel, Timber Racking Wall, Timber Connections, Glulam Beams, Wind Loading, Snow Loading, Outrigger Loading, Sheet Pile Wall, Ground-Bearing Slab, Flitch Beams, Piles and Pile Groups, Raft Foundation, Retaining Wall, Gabion Retaining Wall.

Steel Member Design (design actions) and Steel Beam Analysis & Design (spans, loads and combinations) are separate pages since October 2026, built from one template with the same engine. Together they cover:

- **Sections:** UKB, UKC, PFC (British Steel datasheets); SHS/RHS/CHS, hot-finished (BS EN 10210-2) and cold-formed (BS EN 10219-2); custom welded or rolled I. Historic British sections (November 2026, at Luke's request) from the BCSA *Historical Structural Steelwork Handbook*: 534 beams, columns and channels from Dorman Long's 1887 list to BS 4:1972, converted to metric. Its tables give A, I and the mean thicknesses but no root radii or plastic and torsion constants: W<sub>el</sub> from I, W<sub>pl</sub> by the shape factor of the idealised plates, I<sub>t</sub> and I<sub>w</sub> of the plates (conservative), r = 0.
- **Steel:** S235 to S460 (BS EN 10025), the historic British specifications by thickness from the handbook (BS 15:1948 mild steel, BS 548:1934 and BS 968 of 1943 and 1962 high tensile), or a yield strength given by the engineer (tested or assessed). Picking a pre-1948 historic series with a modern grade turns the grade to a given f<sub>y</sub>, a 1948–68 one to BS 15; the page notes the handbook's advice (its 9.6) to check old members by BS 449 with the stresses of their time, the wrought iron of before about 1910, and the judgement the engineer needs for the steel, its ductility and its connections.
- **Checks:** Class 4 effective sections, web bearing and buckling (EN 1993-1-5 section 6), horizontal (minor-axis) loads in Steel Beam Analysis & Design, and Annex A or Annex B interaction.
- **Buckling lengths (member page):** as factors of L with equal lateral-torsional segments, or given in metres (November 2026, at Luke's request): L<sub>cr,y</sub> and L<sub>cr,z</sub> for axial buckling about each axis and L<sub>LT</sub> for lateral-torsional buckling under M<sub>y</sub> (also the torsional buckling length). An L<sub>LT</sub> shorter than the member is checked between forks at every position along it (the restraints' places are not given) and the worst governs; one longer than the member takes C<sub>1</sub> from the member's moment diagram with M<sub>cr,0</sub> over L<sub>LT</sub>. A length above L counts as a sway mode (C<sub>m</sub> 0.9). The beam page keeps its restraint positions.

Limits:
- Angles and tees are not covered, nor the handbook's compound (plated and double) girders and stanchions, its continental sections, cast or wrought iron.
- Class 4 flanges of I sections and channels, and Class 4 CHS (EN 1993-1-6), are rejected rather than designed.
- Hollow-section wall bearing is not checked.
- Torsion from eccentric loading (for example channels loaded in the web plane) belongs to the torsion module.

Hollowcore Support Beam covers:

- **Sections:** UKB / UKC (planks on the top or bottom flange), ASB (British Steel datasheet, planks on the bottom flange), Slimflor (UKC with a welded bottom plate, planks on the plate).
- **Stages:** 1 planks on one side; 2 both sides with wet topping and construction load on either or both sides (beam unrestrained, P385 torsion, M<sub>cr</sub> with load height and z<sub>j</sub>); 3 final, restrained (6.2.5, 6.2.6, 6.2.8), with an option to check out-of-balance torsion on the beam alone.
- **Local:** plank bearing length, transverse bending of the bearing flange / plate with the coexistent longitudinal stress, Slimflor plate welds.

Limits:

- Non-composite; composite action (P359) is a separate module.
- Slimflor weld prying uses a simple elastic estimate (Q = 6m/b) and should be confirmed by the engineer for unusual plate proportions.
