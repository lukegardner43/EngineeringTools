Engineering Tools

Tower Crane Base

A square gravity base or four-pile cap under a tower crane mast, after CIRIA C761  ·  the manufacturer's loads for each case with C761's partial factors  ·  overturning (EQU), bearing, sliding and slewing torque to BS EN 1997-1 on the effective area with the jib at every angle  ·  presumed bearing, settlement and the mast's tilt  ·  the base's bending, shear, punching and the legs' pull-out to BS EN 1992-1-1  ·  on piles: the pile loads for the pile designer and the cap's beams, shear and punching

Project
Crane loads (characteristic, from the manufacturer)
M, H and F at the top of the base (kNm, kN), T the slewing torque in service. The crane's own weight is its permanent vertical load, usually the out of service F; in service, the rest of F is variable. Out of service and during erection F is all permanent. The cast-in legs from the manufacturer's drawing: where a leg's compression goes into the concrete (C761: the first cross piece) and the depth of its foot plate, both from the top of the base, and how level the legs are set (C761 ±1 mm).
Base
C761 takes 24 kN/m³ for lightly reinforced bases. The mast sits centrally, its legs on the square's axes.
Piles
Four piles at the corners of a square under the mast, the moment on the diagonal (C761 Example 3). The pile loads come out for the pile designer; give the piles’ working capacities (C761 matches them to the SLS loads) or their design resistances to check them. The ground and the tilt are then the pile designer’s: the tool gives the movement the piles may have.
Ground
From the geotechnical design report. The presumed bearing pressure (C761’s prescriptive method) is checked against the characteristic pressure on the effective area. The settlement takes the characteristic pressure on a rigid circle of the effective area (C761 takes E ≈ 3 N60 MPa in sand, 500 cu in stiff clay with ν 0.5) and adds the legs’ level tolerance; the limit is the crane maker’s, often 1 in 500. Leave E blank to skip it. No water table: below the base is assumed dry.
Give the crane's loads, the base and the ground, then Check.
