RC Corbel and Nib Design

Corbels and continuous nibs to BS EN 1992-1-1 + UK NA  ·  strut-and-tie (6.5) with PD 6687-1 Annex B.4 in place of Annex J  ·  nodes, strut slope, tie, links, shear, bearing, hanger steel and the anchorage of the tie at both ends

Partly verifiedTwo published worked examples are reproduced; other arrangements are checked only by hand so far.
Project
Load ULS
Unless the bearing lets the supported member move, allow a horizontal force of 0.2FEd (CCIP-057). Left blank, aH puts HEd on the top face.
Geometry mm
The bearing sits at ac from the support face. A precast bearing is also checked against 10.9.5.2(2): 0.4fcd dry, otherwise fbed up to 0.85fcd.
Concrete and reinforcement
Closed two-leg links: horizontal where ac ≤ 0.5hc (at least 0.5As,main), vertical where ac > 0.5hc and FEd > VRd,c (at least 0.5FEd/fyd), PD 6687-1 B.4.2–B.4.3.
Anchorage of the tie
B.4.4: in the corbel the anchorage runs from the inner face of the loading plate (poor bond); in the support from the bars at the near face to the far face (good bond). Where the straight length falls short the bend must carry the rest, (8.1); the standard mandrel is 4φ up to 16 mm and 7φ above (Table NA.6a). A welded cross bar takes up to 0.5Asfyd per bar (8.6).
