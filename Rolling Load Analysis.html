Rolling Load Analysis

A train of wheel or axle loads rolled across a single or continuous beam  ·  the largest bending moments, shears and reactions anywhere, and where the loads stand for them  ·  design envelopes with the permanent load, and the deflection from the moving loads

Project
Beam
Spans left to right, with pinned supports between them. I is the second moment of area about the bending axis (cm⁴); it matters for continuous beams and for deflection. G: the permanent load on the span (self-weight, rail, finishes), kN/m.
Moving loads (characteristic)
Each load with its distance from the one before (crane wheels, vehicle axles, a trolley). φ multiplies the moving loads for strength, for example the crane factors of BS EN 1991-3 Table 2.4; deflection is from the characteristic loads without it.
Factors and limits
γQ 1.5 for imposed loads (BS EN 1990, UK NA); BS EN 1991-3 Annex A gives 1.35 for crane actions. Deflection: span/600 for crane runway beams (BS EN 1993-6 Table 7.1), span/360 for floors; cantilevers at twice that.
The beam and the loads follow the inputs. Calculate to roll the loads across: envelopes, the largest effects, the reactions and the A4 sheet.
