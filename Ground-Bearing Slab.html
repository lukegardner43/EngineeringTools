Ground-Bearing Slab

Industrial concrete floors on the ground, after Concrete Society TR34 (4th edition)  ·  plain, fabric, steel or macro-synthetic fibre slabs  ·  racking, materials handling and other point loads, single, paired or in fours, internal, at an edge or at a joint with aggregate interlock and dowels (Meyerhof)  ·  punching with the ground’s support  ·  line loads and uniform loads (Hetényi)  ·  fatigue under heavy trucks

Project
Slab
k from plate bearing tests on the subgrade at 1.25 mm settlement (TR34 5.1, 5.6), without enhancement for the sub-base. fR1 to fR4: the mean residual flexural strengths of the notched beam tests (EN 14651) for the fibre and dosage specified. Fabric in the bottom of the slab (A142 142, A193 193, A252 252, A393 393 mm²/m).
Joints
Loads at joints use the edge capacity, raised by the load passed across (7.9): 15% by aggregate interlock at sawn joints and the dowels within 0.9 l either side, never more than the internal capacity. Plate dowels: give the maker’s capacity per dowel.
Point loads (characteristic)
x, y: the contact area (baseplates: the stiff part, d + 4t; 100 × 100 for racking if not known; wheels: from the maker or the load over the tyre pressure). No.: 1, 2 or 4 loads at sx (and sy) centres, each of P. Back-to-back racking uprights: two loads at their spacing. Factors: racking 1.2, other static 1.5, materials handling 1.6 (TR34 7.2).
Line and uniform loads (characteristic)
Line loads (walls, racking runs) and block stacking by Hetényi’s elastic analysis, against the cracking moment with the 1.5 on the concrete (7.11, 7.12). Distance: from the free edge or joint to the line.
