Gabion Retaining Wall

Gabion gravity walls of stepped courses leaning back into the soil  ·  Coulomb thrust on the wall's back plane  ·  sliding, bearing (Annex D) and overturning to BS EN 1997-1 and the UK NA, or by factors of safety  ·  sliding and overturning at every course joint

Partly verifiedThe thrust, sliding and overturning reproduce a published gabion design example; bearing and the course joints are checked only by hand so far.

Design basis
Wall
Course 1 is the base. Sizes in metres; the front offset is from the front of the base course to the front of this one, with the wall set upright. Each course must sit fully on the one below.
α: 6° for standard (39 system) gabions, up to 10° for the 27 system, 0 for a vertical wall. γg: the unit weight of the filled gabion.
Retained soil and loads
The surcharge acts on the retained ground behind the wall. 10 kN/m² is the usual construction load; allow more for plant or traffic near the wall.
Founding soil
The base sits 0.3 to 0.5 m below the ground in front. Base friction: the gabion guides take the founding soil's φ′ from tests; ⅔ φ′ is the older code value. Gabion on gabion: 35° from tests. The allowable pressure is checked against the characteristic toe pressure; leave it blank to skip.
Set out the courses, the ground and the loads, then Check.
