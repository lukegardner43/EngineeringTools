Timber 2D Frame Analysis and Design

Timber roof trusses and portal frames analysed in their plane, every member designed to BS EN 1995-1-1 + UK NA  ·  Fink, Howe, Pratt and king post trusses, parallel-chord trusses, flat and pitched portals  ·  each load combination with its own kmod  ·  compression, tension and bending with buckling in and out of the plane, lateral torsional buckling, shear  ·  the sway imperfection, αcr and second order for portals  ·  deflection

Members mm
Restraint spacing: the distance between restraints to the member’s compression edge out of the plane (battens or purlins on a top chord or rafter, binders on a bottom chord, side rails on a column; fly braces where a rafter’s inner edge is in compression at the knees); blank or 0 for the member’s own length. In the plane each member buckles over its length between joints.
Loads (characteristic) kN/m²
Each × the frame spacing. Wind as the net pressure on each surface (cpe − cpi) × qp, + toward the surface, − suction; on a parallel-chord truss the left-slope value acts on the whole top. Roof imposed (category H, short term) is the UK’s 0.6 kN/m² for roofs without access; the ceiling’s imposed load (medium term) is for a loft or storage over a ceiling.
