Timber Beam

Joists, rafters and beams in solid timber, glulam and LVL, simply supported, to BS EN 1995-1-1 + UK NA  ·  each load combination with its own kmod  ·  bending with lateral buckling, shear with notches, bearing  ·  instantaneous and net final deflection  ·  floor vibration by the UK NA method

Project
Member
Take these from the manufacturer’s current declaration (BS EN 14374): the defaults are the Kerto-S values Porteous and Kermani used. kh = (300/h)s ≤ 1.2; kc,90 = 1.
C and D classes from BS EN 338:2016, glulam from BS EN 14080:2013; γM 1.3 solid timber, 1.25 glulam (UK NA Table NA.3). Service class 1 for heated interiors, 2 for covered but unheated (UK NA Table NA.2).
Span and restraint
L = clear span + one bearing length. Beyond the bearing: how far the member runs past the bearing’s outer edge, which lets the bearing spread there (lef + up to 30 mm, 6.1.5). With the top edge held, the bottom edge is taken as held at the supports only if wind reverses the bending.
Loads (characteristic)
G permanent, Q imposed (of the category above; floors medium term, storage long term, roofs short term), S snow (short term), W wind (instantaneous; tick “up” for suction). Positions in m from the left support (centre of the bearing).
Deflection and vibration
m from the permanent load only (35 kg/m² is about 0.34 kN/m²). B the floor’s width across the joists. Deck E: 18 mm OSB/3 about 4930 N/mm² along its length. kamp 1.05 for simply supported solid joists, 1.15 for glued thin-webbed joists.
