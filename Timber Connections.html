Timber Connections

Nailed, screwed, bolted and dowelled joints to BS EN 1995-1-1 Section 8 + UK NA  ·  timber, plywood, OSB and steel plates in single and double shear  ·  every failure mode with the rope effect  ·  effective number, spacings, splitting, withdrawal and slip

Partly verifiedNails, plywood gussets, withdrawal and coach screws reproduce published examples; bolts, dowels, steel plates and double shear are checked only by hand so far.

Joint
Fasteners
Nails up to 8 mm and screws up to 6 mm (def) take the nail rules; bolts, dowels and bigger screws the bolt rules with embedment at the angle to the grain. fu: about 600 N/mm² for nail wire, 400 for grade 4.6 bolts, 800 for 8.8. The rope effect uses each fastener’s own withdrawal capacity, up to 15 % (smooth nails), 50 % (ring-shank), 100 % (screws), 25 % (bolts) or nothing (dowels) of the yield value.

α: the angle between the force and each member’s grain (0 along, 90 across). For bolts and dowels the embedment follows it (8.31); for nails it does not. h and he are for the splitting check when the force crosses the main member’s grain (8.1.4). A plate counts as thick when it is at least d thick with holes within 0.1d (8.2.3).
Layout in the main member
In a double-shear joint with nails from both sides, n × m is the number from each side. a₁ at 14d or more keeps nef = n for nails (Table 8.1). Leave a spacing blank to skip its check.
Design forces
The whole joint’s ULS forces (for a double-shear joint, the force in the middle member). The load duration is that of the shortest action in the combination (kmod); a panel on timber takes √(kmod,1kmod,2). Axial force is checked for single-shear joints.
