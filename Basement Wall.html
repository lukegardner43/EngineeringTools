Basement Wall

Basement walls propped by the ground floor and the basement slab, to BS EN 1997-1 and BS EN 1992-1-1 with the UK National Annexes  ·  earth and water pressure, surcharges and wall loads  ·  the wall spanning between its props, the footing, bearing, crack control and early-age restraint

Supports
The wall spans from the top of its footing to the ground floor, which props it; the ground behind stands at height H above the underside of the footing. A wall fixed into its footing takes a moment there; a wall pinned at the slab does not.
Project
Geometry mm
H runs from the underside of the footing to the ground behind. The toe points into the basement; the heel is B − toe − wall.
Soil behind
Water levels are heights above the underside of the base. c′ is ignored behind the wall.
Surcharges characteristic
Line and strip loads act parallel to the wall; their pressure follows the elastic solutions for a rigid wall. Wall loads come down the wall from above.
Foundation soil
Concrete and reinforcement
Crack widths: BS 8102 Grade 1 basements commonly take 0.3 mm, Grades 2 and 3 0.2 mm or less. The horizontal bars are checked for early-age restraint: the minimum steel for each face's surface zone at the strength of first cracking (EC2 7.3.2, BS EN 1992-3 7.3.2).
