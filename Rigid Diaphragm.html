Rigid Diaphragm

A storey’s horizontal load shared between its shear walls, cores and braced bays by a rigid floor or roof  ·  the centre of rigidity, and the torsion from the load’s eccentricity  ·  by stiffness, or by racking strength for timber frame walls (PD 6693-1 20.3)  ·  wind torsion by the UK National Annex to BS EN 1991-1-4 (Figure NA.10)

Project
Plan and walls
H: the height over which the walls cantilever (to this floor from the foundations), or the storey height for walls fixed at both ends; drift is the floor’s characteristic movement at its corners against H/limit. The concrete stiffness factor reduces Ecm for cracking (1.0 uncracked, for example 0.5 cracked).
Each wall diaphragm takes the load in proportion to its design racking strength Fi,v,Rd (from the Timber Racking Wall tool), where the floor or roof meets PD 6693-1 Annex E or BS EN 1995-1-1 9.2.3; the torsion from the eccentricity between the wind and the walls’ resistance goes to the walls both ways (20.3 d)).
Walls, cores and braced bays
Origin at the bottom-left corner of the plan. A wall along X resists load in X: its line is y = the value given, from x to x. A wall along Y: x = the value, from y to y. Enter each wall of a core separately. FRd: the wall’s design resistance, if you want it checked.
Horizontal load on this storey (characteristic)
Fk: the storey’s wind (zones D and E) or other horizontal load, acting at the centre of the loaded face; leave the position blank for the middle of the plan. γ 1.5 for wind (BS EN 1990 Table NA.A1.2(B)); 1.0 for loads already factored. Figure NA.10 replaces Figure 7.1 of BS EN 1991-1-4 in the UK (NA.2.23): cpe of zones D and E × 1.3 rising from nothing at one end of each face, which is 0.65F at b/6 from the centre. If F includes the lack-of-correlation factor of 7.2.2(3), give it, so it is taken out of the torsion case.
The plan follows the inputs. Calculate for the wall forces, the checks and the A4 sheet.
