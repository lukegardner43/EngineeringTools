Project
Frame bays in m, left to right
Frame spacing is the distance to the next frame, for the flange width beff (5.3.2.1). The loads you give are per metre of beam in this frame. A braced frame is held sideways at every floor; an unbraced one also takes the wind and the frame imperfection (5.2(7)).
Storeys bottom up; the floor beam is at the top of each storey; mm unless stated
Columns: b across the frame, h in its plane (the dimension that resists the frame moments). Edge columns are the outer two in each storey. Beam shape T, L or R (rectangular); hf is the slab depth. Wind: the characteristic horizontal force at each floor, left to right (it is applied both ways); for unbraced frames only.
Sub-frame one floor with the columns above and below; mm unless stated
The far ends of the columns are held sideways and fixed or pinned against rotation; the floor does not sway. Columns above with height 0: none (a roof). This is The Concrete Centre's sub-frame (TCC21), checked against it.
Loads characteristic, on the beams; self-weight added
Floor: All, or one floor (1 is the lowest). Span: All, the bays only, the cantilevers only, or one span counted from the left (a left cantilever is span 1). a is measured from the left end of the span.
Materials and design basis
δ applies at the internal columns of braced frames; unbraced frames are not redistributed. k = Σ(EI/l) of the columns at the joint over Σ c·(factor)·EI/L of the beams, c = 2 braced or 6 unbraced (5.8.3.2(3)–(5)); at least 0.1, and 0.1 at a fixed base.
