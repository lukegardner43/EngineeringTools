Concealed Plate Glulam Connections

Steel plates slotted into glulam or timber (or on its faces) with a group of dowels or bolts, to BS EN 1995-1-1 + UK NA Section 8 and Annex A and BS EN 1993-1-8  ·  shear, axial force and moment shared over the group, each fastener at its angle to the grain  ·  every shear plane by the Johansen equations  ·  the effective number along the grain, splitting, block shear  ·  spacings  ·  the plates in bearing, shear, bending and tension

Partly verifiedThe group forces, embedment at an angle, the shear-plane resistances, the rope effect and the effective number reproduce a published example (Porteous and Kermani, steel plates on the faces); the slotted-in arrangements, splitting, block shear and the plates are checked only against hand calculations.
Project
Timber member
Steel plates
Slotted plates sit in slots a millimetre or two wider than the plate, the timber split equally either side; with two or three plates the inner pieces default to twice the outer, as they work on both faces. The plate’s own connection to the support (welds, bolts, a column) is not checked here.
Fasteners
Dowels in holes of their own diameter in the timber, d + 1 in the plates; bolts with washers on the outer faces (d + 2 holes). Holding a beam up, put the fasteners low in the depth: splitting (8.1.4) depends on their distance from the top edge.
Design forces ULS
V across the grain (+ when the plate holds the member up, as at a beam end), N along it. The group takes M = V e about its centroid, e from the line of zero moment (the pin, or the face of the support for a pinned connection) to the centroid: g + the centroid’s distance from the timber’s end. kmod from the shortest-duration action in the combination.
