Timber Stud Wall

Load-bearing stud walls to BS EN 1995-1-1 + UK NA  ·  each stud under its share of the vertical load and the wind, held by sheathing or noggings  ·  buckling, lateral buckling, sole plate bearing and the lateral deflection  ·  each load combination with its own kmod

Project
Studs
From the manufacturer’s current declaration (BS EN 14374); the defaults are the Kerto-S values Porteous and Kermani used.
The sole plate is taken of the same material as the studs, on a continuous support (a floor or slab). Service class 2 for the outer walls of most timber frames (inside the breather membrane), 1 for heated internal walls.
Restraint
Sheathing fixed to both faces (boards nailed at close centres) holds each stud against buckling about its thin axis and against lateral buckling. With sheathing on one face only, or plasterboard that may be removed, choose noggings or nothing: lef,z = H/(rows + 1), and lateral buckling over 0.9 × that + 2h (Table 6.1).
Loads (characteristic)
Vertical: compression positive (an uplift from wind negative). Wind pressure on the wall’s face, each stud taking the spacing’s width, simply supported over H. e: how far the vertical load sits off the stud’s centre, across the wall (joists hung from one face, say). G permanent, Q imposed (floors medium term, roofs short term), S snow, W wind.
Lateral deflection
Characteristic combination with wind leading, the vertical load amplifying the stud’s bow and the wind’s deflection (P&K eq. 5.46). EC5 sets no limit for walls: agree one that suits the finishes.
