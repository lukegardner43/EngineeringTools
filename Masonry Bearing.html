Masonry Bearing

Concentrated loads on plain masonry under beams and lintels, to BS EN 1996-1-1 6.1.3 + UK NA  ·  the enhancement β from the position of the load  ·  whether a padstone is needed, and the padstone checked (concrete to BS EN 1992-1-1 6.7) with the masonry under it  ·  spreader beams  ·  the wall at mid-height below

Partly verifiedThe masonry strengths reproduce published examples; the padstone and wall checks are largely by hand so far.
Project
Load at the bearing
The beam’s reaction at this bearing. Gk and Qk are factored by 1.35 and 1.5 (BS EN 1990 6.10).
Wall
aL and aR run from the edges of the bearing to the end of the wall or the edge of an opening; 0 puts the bearing at the end of the wall. hc runs to the underside of the beam, with any padstone included.
Masonry
fb is the normalised mean compressive strength the manufacturer declares (BS EN 772-1), not the declared mean strength of the unit. fk = K fb0.7 fm0.3 with K from UK NA Table NA.4 (fk = K fb0.85 in thin layer mortar). γM from UK NA Table NA.1.
fk from a test, the UK NA, or the manufacturer. The group of the units still decides whether β can be more than 1. γM from UK NA Table NA.1.
Bearing
lb is usually the beam’s flange or breadth, wb how far it sits on the wall. The load acts at the centre of the bearing, so a narrow bearing at one face puts it off the centre line of the wall.
The padstone sits centred on the bearing, moved in to stay within the wall at an end, and centred across the wall. A padstone deeper than 200 mm, longer than 3 lb and as wide as the wall is a spreader beam (6.1.3(7)).
Wall below the load
The concentrated load is spread over lefm at mid-height and added to the other load on the wall there (floors, the wall above, self-weight). Its eccentricity e across the wall gives a moment N e at the top that falls to half at mid-height. The other leaf sets tef = ∛(t₁³ + t₂³); give 0 for a single leaf.
